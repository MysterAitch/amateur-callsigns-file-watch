**Freedom of Information: Internal Review**

Dear Mr James

Thank you for your request for an internal review of your Freedom of
Information request.

On 7 January you asked:

*I would be very grateful if you could provide me a list of all unallocated
UK amateur radio call signs (i.e. those call signs that would be available
to anybody who has just passed a Foundation, Intermediate of Full exam).*

On 19 January Julia Snape replied to you stating that:

*In terms of your latest request, I note that you made exactly the same
request on 11 August 2014 to which we responded on 18 August. Given that it
is less than 5 months since this request and that we provided a full answer
to exactly the same request in November, we have decided that Section 14 of
the Act applies and we will not be providing the information requested. We
consider it has been made too soon after your last request for exactly the
same information and will involve a disproportionate amount of time and
effort to search for.*

On 6 February you asked for an internal review.

On 25 February Tim Smith emailed you and provided all the information you
had originally requested.

On 10 March following the release of all the information requested Jonathan
Pillinger-Cork closed your internal review.

On 25 March you asked for the internal review to be reopened.

I have reviewed your request for information and Ofcom's responses. I am
pleased that Ofcom has now provided all the information you requested.

Ofcom's initial rejection of your request was based on S.14 (2) of the Act,
which states that 'a public authority is not obliged to comply with a
subsequent identical or substantially similar request from that person
unless a reasonable interval has elapsed'. Your previous request for the
same information was received on 11 August and responded to on 18 August,
approximately 5 months prior to your current request.

You argue however that, the request was not a repeat as the information had
changed substantially, that your request was effectively for new
information.

You have suggested in your request for an internal review that the
information you have requested is dynamic, frequently updated and because of
this a reasonable interval between requests would be a month.

You also suggest in your request for an internal review that, given your
knowledge of Ofcom's systems, it would not require a disproportionate amount
on time to compile the list and therefore Ofcom should comply with your
request.

I will deal with these issues in turn.

Your 7 January request was substantially similar to your request in August –
whilst I accept that the information is dynamic, your request was for the
same information and was therefore identical to your earlier request. This
was set out by Ms Snape in her response to you.

**I accept that, due to the nature of the information and the frequent
changes to it, 6 months was too long an interval to insist on between
particular requests.** Whilst I cannot place a minimum time period on repeat
requests, we will take account of the detail you have provided on the nature
of the information when considering future requests.

**Given the dynamic nature of the information, I have concluded that there
would not have needed to be a disproportionate amount of effort undertaken
to comply with your request in this instance.**

In addition you raise the issue of your additional request which asked for
details of the time it would take to comply with your request. As you have
also asked for a separate internal review of this issue, I will deal with
this by separate letter.

As you now have all the information you requested and an explanation of
where Ofcom was incorrect in its initial judgement, I trust your are content
with the outcome of your request.

Yours sincerely,

Graham Howell.
