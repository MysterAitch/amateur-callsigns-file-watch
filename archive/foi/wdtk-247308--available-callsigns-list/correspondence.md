## Overview

- **Asked**: the current list of all unallocated UK amateur radio callsigns
  (the requester's second such request; the first was 2014-08, entry
  wdtk-224333).
- **Withheld** (initially): refused under **s.14(2)** (identical/repeat
  request, ~5 months after the last) — the refusal signposts a **response
  to a similar request published on Ofcom's stakeholders FOI pages, dated
  27 November 2014, "publicly available since 19 November 2014"** (a
  published available-list to cross-reference).
- **Provided**: the full current list, released **mid-review** on
  2015-02-25 by Tim Smith (Data Integrity & Solutions Manager) —
  `Amateur Available Call signs 25 02 2015.xlsx`, three worksheets
  (extents indicative, from declared dimensions):
  - `Foundation` — ~7,004 rows × cols A:H, available-pool
  - `Intermediate` — ~8,751 rows × cols A:H, available-pool
  - `Full` — ~7,283 rows × cols A:H, available-pool
- **Significance** (on the record — see the IR raw extract):
  - The internal review concedes **"6 months was too long an interval to
    insist on between particular requests"** given the dynamic data, and
    that compliance **would not have required disproportionate effort** —
    a cadence precedent for repeat requests against changing callsign data.
  - The requester's IR argument documents the era's exam cadence (advanced
    exams every two months; foundation/intermediate on demand) and repeats
    the plea for Ofcom to publish the list — the second such ask after
    wdtk-224333's.
  - Procedural record: data released mid-review; review "closed" without
    conclusion, challenged (including a public annotation by another WDTK
    user), reopened, and concluded formally.
  - This entry's spreadsheet carries the 8-column Siebel export shape
    (`Country | Current Series | Reference | Value | Type | Product |
    Status | Allocated Flag`) — the first appearance of headers in the
    available-list series.

**2015-01-19 — refusal (Julia Snape, Ofcom):**

> Dear Mr James
>
> Thank you for your email requesting a list of all unallocated UK amateur
> radio call signs which was received on 7 January 2015 and has been
> considered under the Freedom of Information Act 2000 ('the Act').
>
> I would refer you in the first instance to a response to a similar
> request for information sent on 27 November 2014. This has been published
> at:
>
> http://stakeholders.ofcom.org.uk/freedom[-of-information/foi-responses/…]
>
> and will provide information up to that date.
>
> In terms of your latest request, I note that you made exactly the same
> request on 11 August 2014 to which we responded on 18 August. Given that
> it is less than 5 months since this request and that we provided a full
> answer to exactly the same request in November, we have decided that
> Section 14 of the Act applies and we will not be providing the
> information requested. We consider it has been made too soon after your
> last request for exactly the same information and will involve a
> disproportionate amount of time and effort to search for. We consider
> that it is reasonable to request an up to date list after 6 months has
> elapsed since information on this area has been publicly available since
> 19 November 2014.
>
> I hope the information already published is of help.
>
> Kind regards
> Julia Snape

**2015-02-06 — internal-review request (Mr James):**

> Dear Office of Communications,
>
> Please pass this on to the person who conducts Freedom of Information
> reviews.
>
> I am writing to request an internal review of Office of Communications's
> handling of my FOI request 'List of available Amateur Radio Call-signs'.
>
> Thank you for your response.
>
> Although you hold the information you denied my requested based on
> 'section 14'. You didn't include in your response what that meant but I
> have looked it up in the legislation...
>
> 14 Vexatious or repeated requests.
>
> (1)Section 1(1) does not oblige a public authority to comply with a
> request for information if the request is vexatious.
>
> (2)Where a public authority has previously complied with a request for
> information which as made by any person, it is not obliged to comply with
> a subsequent identical or substantially similar request from that person
> unless a reasonable interval has elapsed between compliance with the
> previous request and the making of the current request.
>
> Your response didn't mention whether you denied my request because you
> considered it vexatious or because it was a repeated request but your
> response suggests to me that it was because it was repeated. I will
> continue on that basis.
>
> There are two things to consider.
> 1. Whether my request was "identical or substantially similar" to a
> previous request.
> 2. And if so, whether a "reasonable interval has elapsed".
>
> My request didn't explicitly state that I wanted the CURRENT list of
> available call signs, but I think that is a reasonable interpretation.
> Your response suggests that you were considering generating an up to date
> list.
>
> The current list of available call signs will change maybe as often as
> every day, likely every week and certainly every two months. New call
> signs are issued when people pass the RCF exams. The advanced exams are
> held every two months. The foundation and intermediate exams are held on
> demand as and when candidates and examiners are ready.
>
> So, my request was not for the same information, but for a different set
> of information - the CURRENT list of available call signs.
>
> You also say that it "... will involve a disproportionate amount of time
> and effort to search for." I was surprised at this as your web site
> checks the availability of call signs online - so I infer that there is a
> database of these call signs and typically a query against a database is
> a simple task taking very little time. Especially if you have done it
> before.
>
> I asked in a separate request how long it took you to create this list
> and was told that you didn't hold that information. Again I am surprised,
> that you don't record how long your staff take to answer FoI queries.
> Isn't this part of your monthly reporting? Maybe someone can remember how
> it took them even though they didn't write it down (and thus be in the
> scope of the FoI). If it takes a "disproportionate amount of time" to
> repeat a simple query against a database I suggest that you have a
> problem with your IT system.
>
> In conclusion:
> 1. My request was not a repeat - I wanted new information - the CURRENT
> list of available call signs.
> 2. It would not take a disproportionate amount of time to generate an up
> to date list.
> 3. The information changes more frequently than every six months. I think
> a "reasonable interval" is one month.
>
> On a wider point. I am not the only one asking for this information. For
> those taking the amateur radio exams choosing their call sign is an
> important part of getting licenced. Many people's first choice call signs
> have already been allocated so it is useful to be able to see what is
> left. I suggested in my first request for this information that you
> publish the up to date list and I again urge you to do so. It would be a
> useful service to new and upgrading radio amateurs.
>
> A full history of my FOI request and all correspondence is available on
> the Internet at this address:
> https://www.whatdotheyknow.com/request/l[ist_of_available_amateur_radio_3]
>
> Thank you,
>
> Mr James
