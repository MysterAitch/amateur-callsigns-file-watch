Dear Mr Eckersley

**Freedom of Information: Right to know request**

Thank you for your request for information for the policy and guidelines used
when considering whether a person may be issued with an 'old' call sign,
together with a list of reasons each of the last 20 such applicants were
issued with an old call sign. This was received on 30 January and has been
considered under the Freedom of Information Act 2000 (the Act).

Our policy and guidelines on the transfer of call signs are available on our
website:
http://licensing.ofcom.org.uk/radiocommunication-licences/amateur-radio/guidance-for-licensees/transfer-radio-call-signs/

The availability of Special Contest Call signs (which are also short-form but
available only via a formal variation to the licence) is set out on the
application:
http://licensing.ofcom.org.uk/binaries/spectrum/amateur-radio/apply-for-a-licence/ofw286.pdf

The format of regular call signs is published on the website:
http://licensing.ofcom.org.uk/radiocommunication-licences/amateur-radio/guidance-for-licensees/amateur-radio-call-signs/

Please see the following links for relevant FAQs:

http://licensing.ofcom.org.uk/radiocommunication-licences/amateur-radio/faq/759473

http://licensing.ofcom.org.uk/radiocommunication-licences/amateur-radio/faq/759480

Attached is table showing each of the last 20 applications where an old call
sign was reallocated. These fall into the 'club licence' category where the
chairperson of the club is deceased and the remaining members have requested
to carry on the club and have therefore gained consent to do so. The names of
the call sign holders have been withheld under Section 40 of the Act which
protects personal information from being disclosed. Section 40 is an absolute
exemption under the Act and does not require a public interest test.

By way of background Ofcom are keen that stations maintain a constant identity
and that includes amateur radio club stations. If a club were originally
licensed (via an individual) using an old call sign, then it would be our
preference for the club to continue to use the call sign, even though the
original licensee may have died.

Ofcom licenses amateur clubs by granting a licence to an individual (a
'natural person') but on behalf of a club. That is because the constitution of
clubs can vary from one club to another and we require clear accountability.

For further information on this please refer to our consultation on amateur
radio licences - see paras 2.32 to 2.41:
(http://stakeholders.ofcom.org.uk/binaries/consultations/amateur-radio-licence/summary/condoc.pdf).
We set out our decision in paras 3.17 to 3.22 of the subsequent Statement
(http://stakeholders.ofcom.org.uk/binaries/consultations/amateur-radio-licence/statement/Updating_the_Amateur_Radio_Licence.pdf).

If you are looking to have a callsign transferred or have any further
questions on this topic, then please email the team responsible on
spetrum.licensing@ofcom.org.uk

If you would like to make a further freedom of information request then
please email information.requests@ofcom.org.uk and quote the reference number
above.

Julia Snape
