## Overview

- **Asked**: (1) every callsign with a database entry, with status,
  licence type, reservation duration and allocation date — "the status
  information that you use"; (2) the policy/procedures for reserving
  unsuitable or inappropriate callsigns, with those callsigns identified.
- **Provided**: `All Call Signs Assigned plus forbidden.xlsx` — all
  allocated or reserved callsigns with licence type (~139,758 rows), plus
  the forbidden-suffixes list (~1,466 rows) — the oldest full register
  snapshot held by this archive.
- **Withheld**: reservation durations and allocation dates, under s.12
  (18-hour/£450 limit). No availability list — not because of cost but
  because none exists (see below).
- **Significance** (all on the record — see the raw extract):
  - **The algorithm statement**: "Due to a system change, the assignment
    of call signs is now done using an algorithm rather than 'grabbing'
    from a list" — the definitive explanation for the end of the
    availability-list era, and a pointer to the July 2016 historic list
    (FOI 285990, recovered separately from the web archive).
  - **Club/Full indistinguishability**: "we do not know if the call signs
    were allocated to a Full or Club licence" — a standing semantics
    caveat for the licence-type column of every register snapshot.
  - **Forbidden-callsigns provenance**: no formal policy exists —
    "conventional practice" excludes potentially offensive or
    bullying-prone (English) words, and the list "does change over time,
    as taste and social tolerance change"; internationally, Radio
    Regulations Art 19.46 (signal confusion, e.g. SOS) and the ITU-R
    M.1172 Q-codes are "programmed not to allow these as suffixes" — the
    authoritative basis for the forbidden-suffixes reference data.
  - The plain `337399` reference (vs the Siebel `1-XXXXXXXXX` format on
    every earlier letter) marks the 2016 reference-format transition; the
    acknowledgement's `ref:_00D58H42o…` string is a Salesforce artefact.

## Exchange (verbatim; relay email addresses and signature boilerplate omitted)

**2016-09-07 — request (Nan Smith):**

> Dear Office of Communications,
>
> 1. Please provide me with a list of ALL amateur radio call signs that
> OFCOM has a database entry for and their current status such as
> Available, Allocated, Reserved, Historic, etc, what type of licence they
> have been allocated to, how long they are reserved for, when they were
> allocated etc. Please provide the status information that you use rather
> than trying to fit my examples.
>
> I understand that with the UK's allocation of 3 single character prefixes
> and various two character prefixes (for overseas territories) there are
> potentially a huge number of call signs. I assume that you don't keep
> entries for each possible callsign but just the ones that you have
> specifically allocated, reserved etc and those that are currently
> available. This is the list I am requesting.
>
> I note that the information commissioner does not consider searching a
> database as creating new information.
>
> 2. Please can you provide me with a copy of the policy and/or procedures
> for reserving unsuitable or inappropriate callsigns from allocation. (eg
> ones that spell out offensive words). I expect the actual call signs that
> are so reserved to be identified in the list from part 1 above.
>
> Yours faithfully,
>
> Nan Smith
