Reference: 337399
Date: 29 September 2016
Julia Snape, Information requests

**Freedom of Information: Right to know request**

Thank you for your request for information dated 7 September for a list of
all amateur radio call signs that Ofcom holds in its database, the type of
licence, how long they are reserved for and when they were allocated along
with the policy or procedures for reserving unsuitable or inappropriate call
signs from allocation. This has been considered under the Freedom of
Information Act 2000 ('the Act').

Please find attached a list of all call signs that are allocated or reserved
with the type of licence. For the missing licence type, there is a published
regime which denotes the call sign prefix for each licence product:

http://licensing.ofcom.org.uk/radiocommunication-licences/amateur-radio/guidance-for-licensees/amateur-radio-call-signs/737317

For Club licences, the call signs are the same regime as for Full licences.
Therefore, we do not know if the call signs were allocated to a Full or Club
licence.

We do not hold a list of call signs that are available. Due to a system
change, the assignment of call signs is now done using an algorithm rather
than "grabbing" from a list. We have a historic available call sign list
which was disclosed in July 2016 (but is now likely to be out of date) and
which can be found here:
http://stakeholders.ofcom.org.uk/freedom-of-information/foi-responses/2016/july2016/285990/

We are unable to provide how long the call signs are reserved for and when
they were allocated as this would take the total time spent on your request
over the 18-hour limit. Under section 12 of the Act, we are not obliged to
comply with a request if it would exceed the 'appropriate limit', as set out
in the Act. The appropriate limit for Ofcom is £450, calculated at a rate of
£25 per person per hour; this equates to 18 hours of time.

We do not hold a policy on reserving unsuitable or inappropriate call signs
for allocation. However, as a matter of conventional practice we do not
issue call signs or parts of call signs that might spell out (English) words
that we think are likely to be generally offensive or which may lead to
undue on-air bullying of the licensee. You will find a list of these
reserved call signs within the attachment as above. It does change over
time, as taste and social tolerance change.

In addition to the list of potentially offensive call signs, we are required
by Art 19.46 et seq of the Radio Regulations not to allow call signs that
might be confused with internationally accepted signals. That is therefore
an international obligation. For example, 'SOS'. In addition, there is an
international list of so-called 'Q-Codes'. These are three-letter codes that
mean something. For example, 'QSL' means 'Are you receiving' (when
transmitted first) and 'Yes, I am receiving' (when sent as a reply). You can
find further information here:
https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/m/R-REC-M.1172-0-199510-I!!PDF-E.pdf.
Our licensing system has been programmed not to allow these as suffixes.

I hope this information is helpful.

Yours sincerely

Julia Snape
