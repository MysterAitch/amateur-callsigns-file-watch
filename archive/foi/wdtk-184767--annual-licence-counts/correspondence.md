## Overview

- **Asked**: the annual number of licences held (any class) for both the
  amateur radio service and business radio, each year from 1993 to date.
- **Provided**: partially — `Number of licences Coleman.pdf` supplies
  **financial years 2003/04 → 2012/13 only** ("from 2003, the date of
  Ofcom's inception"), and the letter's own wording is licences **issued**,
  not *held* as asked (see `raw-extract-number-of-licences-coleman.md` for
  the full letter and consolidated table).
- **Withheld**: **1993→2003, refused under s.12 FOIA** (cost limit: £450 /
  £25·hour; Ofcom estimated more than 18 hours), with a
  neither-confirm-nor-deny on whether pre-2003 information exists in its
  archives at all. The follow-up about the 2004/5–2005/6 anomaly was
  answered substantively (below).
- **Significance / interpretation caveats (on the record, from the
  follow-up)**:
  - Pre-lifetime-licensing "amateur" figures are NOT amateur-only: they
    include CB and Maritime (Ship Radio / Ship Portable) licences, because
    the Radio Licensing Centre (RLC, Bristol — a third-party contractor)
    reported only an aggregate across licence products. This explains the
    apparent ~5× spike in 2004/5–2005/6 that the requester queried.
  - 2012/13 amateur licence count: 28,041 (class breakdown not given by
    FOI; Ofcom signposted its then-published monthly statistics at
    licensing.ofcom.org.uk — corroborating the monthly publication surface
    also evidenced by wdtk-174543).

**2013-12-12 — follow-up (Nige Coleman):**

> Dear Julia Snape,
>
> Thank you for the information it is very helpful. I apologise for
> pursuing this further but there is an enormous divergence in the number
> of licences held in 2004/5 and 2005/6; the other years show a fairly
> consistent level of twenty to thirty thousand but these two years have
> five times as many and 06/7 still rather higher than average.
>
> Does Ofcom have any idea why there should have been such an enormous
> jump in numbers, which then halve in 2006/7 and return to the more
> familiar level thereafter. The sudden rise in numbers pre-dates the
> introduction of the on-line lifetime licence which might otherwise have
> offered some explanation.
>
> If this information is not readily at hand, please do not spend any time
> on it.
>
> Finally, for the most recent year (12/13), are you able to break down the
> 28041 amateur licences into the three classes of foundation, intermediate
> and full? Thank you in advance.
>
> Yours sincerely,
>
> Nige Coleman

**2014-01-09 — follow-up answer (Julia Snape, Ofcom):**

> Dear Mr Coleman,
>
> Thank you for your further email dated 12 December 2013..
>
> The figures provided for Amateur Radio Licences on issue, prior to the
> introduction of the lifetime licensing regime, include statistical
> information relating to Citizens Band (CB) Radio and also Maritime (Ship
> Radio / Ship Portable) Radio Licences. Unfortunately, as the
> administration of these licences were handled by a third party contractor
> i.e. the Radio Licensing Centre (RLC) in Bristol, Ofcom only received an
> overall figure of all licence products as opposed to a comprehensive
> breakdown.
>
> As for your secondary query. Ofcom publishes monthly statistics on the
> number of Amateur Radio Licence on issue, therefore should you wish to
> obtain a breakdown of licences on issue by licence class we would
> recommend you visit the following link
> [licensing.ofcom.org.uk — Amateur Radio area of the Ofcom website]
> which can be found within the Amateur Radio area of the Ofcom website.
>
> We trust the above information helps.
>
> Kind regards
>
> Julia
