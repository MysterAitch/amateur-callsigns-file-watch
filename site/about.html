About this site

An independent mirror of the UK amateur-radio callsign register and the Freedom-of-Information disclosures about it — built so operators, researchers and the curious can look up, browse and cross-check the public record. It is not an official service.

Not affiliated with Ofcom

This is an independent project. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or operated by Ofcom, the Radiocommunications Agency, the RSGB, or any other authority. It mirrors data that those bodies have published; it does not speak for them. It is not an authoritative source and must not be relied on for licensing, legal or enforcement decisions — for those, consult Ofcom directly.

What this is

The UK amateur-radio callsign register is public data. Ofcom publishes a periodic open-data export of it, and — through Freedom-of-Information requests over more than a decade — has released a series of snapshots, availability lists and statistical breakdowns. Those releases are scattered across Ofcom's disclosure log, the WhatDoTheyKnow request archive and the UK Government Web Archive, in changing formats, and they are not always easy to find or compare.

This site gathers them into one place. It preserves each source verbatim (the original bytes are archived), derives a normalised, component-parsed view for searching and joining, and publishes the lot as a browsable, queryable mirror. You can look up a single callsign, browse a whole publication, compare publications over time, or run your own SQL over the databases — see the lookup and Explore pages.

Why it exists

The guiding idea is epistemic honesty: to show what the record actually says, and — just as importantly — what it does not. A callsign missing from a register export is not proof it is free; a status of "Reserved" does not say why, or for whom; a "declared-complete" export has been observed silently dropping tens of thousands of rows. The mirror is built to surface those limits rather than paper over them, so that absence is never mistaken for evidence. The glossary defines the recurring terms in plain language.

How current the data is

The searchable register reflects whatever Ofcom last published, mirrored on the mirror's most recent deploy. Ofcom's open-data export is not a live feed and is published only periodically, so the snapshot here can lag the real register by weeks. The exact publication and mirror dates are shown near the top of the lookup page and on every entry in the dataset index; the FOI disclosures each carry their own vintage. Older publications remain archived, so the record can be read historically as well as at its latest state.

Reporting errors

Corrections, questions and suggestions are welcome. Because the raw sources are preserved unchanged, an error is usually either in Ofcom's published data (which the mirror faithfully reproduces) or in the mirror's own normalisation, parsing or reference data (which can be fixed here). Please open an issue on the GitHub repository, where the full source, provenance and change history also live.
