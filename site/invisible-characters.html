Invisible characters, and how we show them

Some published data contains characters you cannot see on screen. They can make two entries that look identical actually differ. This page explains what they are, why they matter, and how this site makes them visible.

what they are

What "invisible characters" are

Computers store text as a long list of numbered characters. This numbering scheme is called Unicode, and it covers not just letters and digits but also spaces and other marks that take up room without showing a shape. A few turn up in register data:

  • an ordinary space — for example a stray space at the end of a callsign;
  • a non-breaking space — a special kind of space (often written NBSP) that looks the same as an ordinary one; and
  • the replacement character (numbered U+FFFD, usually shown as �) — the mark that appears when text is converted between formats and a character could not be carried across.

why they matter

Why they matter

Two entries can look exactly the same on screen yet differ in their hidden characters — for example G0ABC and G0ABC (the second has a trailing space). If we quietly deleted the hidden character, we would hide a real difference in the source. If we ignored it, searches and cross-links would fail, because to a computer the two are not the same text.

So this site does neither. It keeps every entry exactly as published, and separately shows the tidied-up canonical form it uses to match and link records. Both are shown together, so you can see precisely what the source contained.

how we show them

How we make them visible

Where an entry contains a character you cannot see, we replace it with a visible marker so you can tell it is there:

  • a space or non-breaking space is shown as ␠ (or written out as [SP] / [NBSP]);
  • the exact bytes of an entry can also be shown as hexadecimal in the Ledger's layer-anatomy view, which is the unambiguous evidence that two look-alike entries really differ.

The marker is only a way of showing you the character — the stored data keeps the original exactly.
